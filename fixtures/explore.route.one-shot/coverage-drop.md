Wren had gone up before her. She found him on the landing with his chin on the top stair and his eyes already on the door, and she had not yet stood, or spoken, or so much as looked at the boots by the range. There was no clock in the kitchen. He had taken the hour from her the way he took the weather, out of some change in her that she could not have named, and carried it up ninety stairs to wait for her there.

The eighty-fourth was loose. It had been loose when she came and it would be loose when she left, and between those two facts she had learned to put her weight on the outer edge, where the iron met the wall, without any part of her deciding to. Wren took it in one long stride that skipped it altogether. He had worked that out for himself, the first winter, and she had watched him do it and said nothing, and that had been the shape of most of what passed between them since.

The lamp room was gold when she came into it, because she had lit it at dusk and a lamp lit at dusk does not need lighting again at one. What it needed was tending. The wick had grown ragged and she cut it back square with the shears from the sill; the float had sunk a finger's width and she fed the reservoir until the brass line held it; the panes had taken the first salt of the night and she gave each one her breath and then the rag. None of it was hers to arrange. The manual had settled the sequence before she was born, and her hands had learned it so well that they no longer consulted her.

The weight was where the counting lived. Sixty turns of the crank, spoken, each one, because she had once wound fifty-eight and spent the small hours listening for the beam to slow. It did not slow. The clockwork accepted the weight and paid it out grain by grain, and the lens went round over the water at the pace it had kept since the spring of 1908.

The log had six columns and had never been five. She filled them by lamplight in the order they were ruled: wind, north-west and fresh; glass, down a little; sea, moderate; and under remarks the word nothing, spelled out, with the boat a month out and a month of nothing the ordinary size of it. A dash could have come from any hand. The word could only have come from the keeper, and the keeper was on the point.

She spoke the stairs going down, ninety-one, for the dog rather than for the number, and spoke them again coming up at four, when the glass had stopped falling and the sea had become something she would want a word for once it was light. She did not hunt for the word. She stood in the gold with her hands at her sides and the dog's weight against her boots, and the night went on being what it was, and it was enough, and there was nobody to say so to, and that was part of what made it so.
