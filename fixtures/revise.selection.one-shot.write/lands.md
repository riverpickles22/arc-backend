The lamp room took her in the order it always took her. Wick first, trimmed square with the shears that lived on the sill and nowhere else. Then the reservoir, paraffin poured until the float rode where the brass line said it should. Then the glass, which was never clean enough and was cleaned anyway, one pane at a time, her breath on it and the rag after. Then the match, and the mantle catching, and the room going from grey to gold between one breath and the next.

The drive weight had to be wound. She put her back into the crank and counted the turns aloud, sixty, because a weight wound short ran out in the small hours and a light that stopped turning was a light that lied. Below the crank the clockwork took the weight and began, without hurry, to spend it.
