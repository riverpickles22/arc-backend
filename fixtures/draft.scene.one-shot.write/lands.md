The wind had gone round in the night and come back wrong. Ines felt it in the door before she felt it on the stairs, a push where there had been a pull, and she stood a moment with her hand flat on the wood, listening to a house she had heard every night for two years say something slightly different.

Wren was at the landing. He had not been called.

In the lamp room the reservoir read a finger low. She put the dip stick in a second time because a keeper who wrote down what she expected was a keeper who would one day write down a lie, and the second reading was the same as the first. A finger low, on a night when the book said full.

She trimmed the wick square. She poured, and watched the float come up to the brass line and stop where it should stop, and she did not think about the finger until the weight was wound and the clockwork had it.

Then the log. Date, hour, wind, glass, sea, remarks. She wrote the wind as it was and the glass as it was, and in remarks, where most nights she wrote nothing out in full, she wrote: oil down one finger at eight, cause not known. Then she sat with the pen over the page a while longer, and did not add anything to it.

Below her the light went round. It did not care what the book said.
